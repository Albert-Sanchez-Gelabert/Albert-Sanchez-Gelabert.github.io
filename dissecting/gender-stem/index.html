The study we are opening
Gender and STEM: academic over-selection and stratified university entry

Sánchez-Gelabert & Simarro Rodríguez · 2026

READ THE MANUSCRIPT ↗
STUDY 02 / EDITORIAL DRAFT

An editorial version grounded in the accepted manuscript. Charts shown here report published totals or methodological relationships; no synthetic student-level coordinates are presented.

Not a simulation and not a results explorer. One research process, opened up.

01 / 07THE QUESTION

What are we really asking?

Gender gaps in STEM are often described as differences in participation. This study asks a different question: what social and academic entry conditions are associated with those patterns?

The research question shifts from counting men and women in STEM to locating degree subfields within a stratified university-entry space.

02 / 07PREVIOUS WORK

What was already known?

Work on gendered STEM participation offers important explanations, while research on educational stratification examines unequal routes and resources. Bringing them together reveals a question that broad comparisons tend to miss.

Previous research motivates the decision to study fields, routes and social positions together; it does not establish that interests or stereotypes are unimportant.

03 / 07THE CONCEPTS

Why go beyond gender?

Gender remains central, but it is not the only axis of inequality. An intersectional orientation prompts us to consider how gender is situated alongside social origin, educational trajectory and life-course conditions.

Looking beyond gender does not mean replacing it: it means analysing where gender becomes salient within wider combinations of advantages and constraints.

04 / 07THE DATA

Who do the records describe?

The administrative file covers university applicants, but the analytical population is deliberately narrower. Defining who is included is part of the research design, not merely data cleaning.

The conclusions concern first-time entrants through the two main access routes in the Catalan university system during 2024–25—not every prospective STEM student.

05 / 07THE MEASURE

What counts as STEM?

Here the central measurement decision is a classification, not a single indicator. A binary STEM/non-STEM label helps summarise participation, but it can erase very different degree subfields.

The 23 AQU Catalunya degree subfields preserve meaningful differences; the STEM/non-STEM binary is a secondary aggregation with explicitly chosen boundaries.

06 / 07THE METHODOLOGICAL STRATEGY

How can we map complexity?

The analytical question is relational and exploratory: how do categorical characteristics occur together, which dimensions organise these associations and where do degree subfields fall within the resulting space?

Multiple correspondence analysis (MCA/ACM) builds a factorial space from active student characteristics. Clusters describe recurring profiles; fields are projected afterwards as illustrative categories.

07 / 07THE CONCLUSIONS

What can we conclude?

The findings become intelligible only after following the question, the classification and the analysis. They concern unequal configurations of university entry, not intrinsic differences between men and women.

Women entering several technical, experimental and biological STEM subfields are concentrated in academically advantaged profiles; men enter STEM from a wider variety of entry positions.
