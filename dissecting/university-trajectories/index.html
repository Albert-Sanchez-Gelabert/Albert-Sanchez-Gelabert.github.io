The study we are opening
Patterns of university progression and social inequalities: delving into complex trajectories in higher education

Troiano & Sánchez-Gelabert · 2026

READ THE PAPER ↗
ABOUT THIS STUDY

A guided look at the research decisions behind a published study of university progression.

Not a tutorial, and not a results explorer. Just one study, opened up.

01 / 07THE QUESTION

Where did the question come from?

The Complex Trajectories project examined the different paths students take through university, beyond graduation or dropout. This study addresses a more specific question: how does academic performance change over time?

The article zooms in on one specific part of that larger question: how academic performance develops over time and how these patterns relate to social and academic characteristics.
IN DETAIL
02 / 07PREVIOUS WORK

What did we know before?

Previous research on university pathways and analyses developed within the project offered different ways to examine students’ experiences. These contributions helped us focus on one dimension: how academic performance changes over time.

The literature helps define the question and the possible methods. It does not prescribe a single technique.
IN DETAIL
03 / 07THE CONCEPTS

What counts as a trajectory?

Before measuring anything, we had to decide what we meant by a complex trajectory. We began with a working definition, encountered cases that were hard to classify, and distinguished several observable dimensions.

A definition sorts experiences into categories. Its boundaries will always matter for borderline cases.
IN DETAIL
04 / 07THE DATA

Who enters the study?

Administrative data are extensive, but a dataset is not automatically a study population. Researchers decide when to start observing, which institutions are comparable and what to do with incomplete records.

A new-entry cohort provides a shared starting point; restricting the institutional setting helps comparability but reduces scope.
IN DETAIL
05 / 07THE MEASURE

How do we measure progression?

A researcher needs an observable indicator for “academic progression”. Here the study uses the percentage of enrolled credits successfully completed each year — a meaningful choice, but not the only possible one.

A percentage measures success relative to workload. It does not necessarily measure absolute pace, nor reveal why credits were not completed.
IN DETAIL
06 / 07THE METHODOLOGICAL STRATEGY

Why this methodological strategy?

We needed to answer two different questions: first, identify patterns in the development of academic performance; then, examine how those patterns relate to student characteristics and fields of study. The methods followed from those questions.

No single technique is best for every kind of trajectory. Different methods highlight different aspects, and every analytical choice shapes what we can interpret.
IN DETAIL
07 / 07THE CONCLUSIONS

What can we claim — and what can’t we?

The final article is a compact account of selected results and arguments. Looking inside it also means looking at how far the evidence reaches and what it does not resolve.

The results support patterns and associations within a defined population, not a causal explanation for every individual trajectory.
