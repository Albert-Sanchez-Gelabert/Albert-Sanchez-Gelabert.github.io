00 / OVERVIEW

A study.
From the inside.

Before a researcher can measure an attitude, someone has to decide what that attitude means — and how to ask about it. This study lets us follow those decisions from an international questionnaire to two empirical dimensions.

Follow the research question, an adapted school survey and the selection of ten items. See what a measure includes — and what it deliberately leaves out.

THE STUDY WE ARE OPENING
La actitud del alumnado frente a la escuela

Valls, Sánchez-Gelabert & Troiano · 2023

READ THE PAPER ↗
WHERE THE QUESTIONNAIRE CAME FROM

The 2023 paper uses data from the International Study of City Youth (ISCY), a longitudinal study. A 2010 international project proposal, a Melbourne questionnaire proof and a revised Catalan draft make parts of the instrument’s development visible.

The questionnaire materials illuminate the instrument’s evolution; they do not provide a complete history of who wrote each item or every revision.

01 / 07THE QUESTION

Can we measure an attitude?

A student can obtain good grades while feeling distant from school — or value school while struggling academically. So how can we study the attitude itself, rather than substitute results or behaviour for it?

The article investigates school attitudes and their relationship to subsequent educational pathways. Its first decision is to define the attitude before selecting the survey items used to represent it.
IN DETAIL
02 / 07PREVIOUS WORK

First decide what belongs to the concept.

The paper revisits theories of school identification, especially Fernández Enguita and Bernstein, and draws a boundary between how students value school, what they do at school and the results they achieve.

The article uses expressive and instrumental identification as two analytical dimensions. School conduct and academic performance remain relevant, but they are not built into the definition of attitude.
IN DETAIL

An important boundary

ATTITUDE

How students value and feel about school.

BEHAVIOUR

What students do — such as disciplinary incidents or absences.

ACHIEVEMENT

What students attain — including assessments and results.

03 / 07THE CONCEPTS

One concept. Two dimensions.

The same word — “attitude” — can refer to distinct forms of identification. The study uses a sociological distinction to decide what the questionnaire has to capture.

Expressive identification concerns students’ emotional relationship with school and its culture. Instrumental identification concerns the value assigned to school and learning as a means to future opportunities.
IN DETAIL
04 / 07THE DATA

The questionnaire had a history.

The Barcelona survey did not start as a blank page for this 2023 article. ISCY was an international project with a common research agenda and locally adapted instruments. We can trace some items across successive documents.

The 2013–2014 Barcelona wave surveyed 2,056 students in the fourth year of compulsory secondary education at 27 schools. The article also uses follow-up information about educational pathways collected until 2017.

2023 · Analytical selection

05 / 07THE MEASURE

From a question to a measure.

The published measure uses ten items drawn from the Barcelona questionnaire: nine from question 17 and one from question 19. The items do not all ask about the same thing — and do not all use the same response scale.

The study selects six expressive and four instrumental items. Questions 17 and 19 are response batteries, not single items; each statement inside a battery provides a separate observed variable.
IN DETAIL

Items 1–5 and 7–10 come from Barcelona question 17 (four agreement responses). Item 6 comes from question 19 (five school-rating responses). Wording below follows the Catalan instrument, translated into English for this page.

06 / 07THE METHODOLOGICAL STRATEGY

From two axes to five profiles.

Once the two dimensions have been constructed, the authors examine how they combine among students. An empirical classification identifies five profiles, rather than treating every pupil as simply “for” or “against” school.

A preliminary MCA and classification informed the regrouping of the two negative agreement categories. PCA then supported two dimensions, and cluster analysis produced five profiles; subsequent models explored links with educational pathways.

07 / 07THE CONCLUSIONS

The measure shapes what we can conclude.

The article finds that attitudes alone explain relatively little of later educational pathways, while social background, achievement and behaviour remain important. That result sends us back to the definition of attitude.

Keeping attitudes, performance and behaviour separate makes their relationships visible — but the authors also acknowledge that these dimensions interact and are difficult to separate completely in practice.
