01 / The decision

Same grades, different futures?

Getting into university is a major decision. But it isn't the last.

Students enter university with expectations about their chances of success. By the end of the first year, they have something they did not have before: information about how well they are doing.

When things go well, the gap is small.

03 / What it means

The same setback. Different ways forward.

Academic results are new information. But students may not have the same resources to respond to them.

When a first year goes badly, students may rethink whether the degree is right for them, whether another route is available or whether continuing is feasible. This is where compensatory advantage offers a sociological interpretation.

Expectations“Can I still succeed?”

Results may prompt students to reassess their chances of completing the degree.

Information“What other routes exist?”

Knowledge of alternatives and familiarity with the university system can differ.

Resources“Can I afford to try again?”

Support and the costs of changing direction can make options more or less feasible.

These are plausible explanatory mechanisms, not resources measured as the direct causes of each student's decision in the study.

When do these inequalities fade? As performance improves, differences in predicted dropout narrow. Around 70% of credits passed, the study no longer detects statistically significant gaps in dropout — not an exact threshold, and not proof that all pathways become identical.

04 / Your sociological insight

Inequality doesn't end at the university gate.

The discovery

When academic results are good, social differences in dropout are small. When things go wrong, different ways forward emerge.

Getting into university is one socially structured transition. Decisions made after the first year can be another. New evidence about academic performance meets the expectations, information and resources students bring with them.

Educational inequality is not only about who gets in. It is also about what becomes possible after a setback.
