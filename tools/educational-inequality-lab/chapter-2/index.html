What happens when university meets the rest of life?

Higher education has welcomed a more diverse student population. But does expanding access mean everyone has the same opportunity to stay?

TWO CHANGES · ONE QUESTION↗
↗
WHO GOES TO UNIVERSITY?

More diverse students.

As higher education expanded, students began entering at different ages, through different pathways, and with different commitments beyond university.

HOW DOES UNIVERSITY WORK?

More continuous participation.

Teaching reforms placed more emphasis on regular participation, group work and continuous assessment, making attendance and day-to-day engagement increasingly important.

Continuous assessmentGroup workRegular participation
THE SOCIOLOGICAL QUESTION

When students have different lives but studying demands time and continuity, does everyone have the same chance of persisting?

Context: Elias, Masjuan & Sánchez-Gelabert (2012). This framing does not imply that the Bologna reforms caused the differences shown below.

02 / FROM PERFORMANCE TO PERSISTENCE

Same academic performance. Same chance of staying?

Let's follow the 1,508 students who passed virtually all their first-year credits in one university cohort. Most fit the traditional student profile. Others entered later or spent more time in paid work. What happened next?

THE DISCOVERY

Even among students with virtually identical levels of academic performance, non-traditional students were more likely to leave.

WHAT THIS ADDS

The age of entry matters for the pattern we observe. Among very high-performing students, older profiles show higher dropout even when working hours are below 15 per week.

The two older high-performance groups are small (n=63 and n=78). These are descriptive rates, not causal estimates. The horizontal tracks use a common 0–100% scale.

06 / YOUR SOCIOLOGICAL INSIGHT

University has become more diverse. But equal access is not equal opportunity to complete.

THE SOCIOLOGICAL DISCOVERY

Doing well academically and being able to continue studying are not necessarily the same thing.

01 · DROPOUT

Equal performance, different outcomes.

In the UAB cohort, dropout varied between student profiles even within the highest academic-performance band.

02 · GRADUATION

Educational paths have a life course.

In a second Catalan cohort, graduation probabilities were associated with age at entry and employment status.

THE QUESTION TO TAKE AWAY

If universities welcome people at different moments of life, have their ways of teaching and supporting students kept pace?

The two empirical studies use different cohorts and outcomes: dropout and graduation cannot be combined into one rate here. These associations do not prove that age, work, institutional reforms or other responsibilities caused individual dropout. The 2012 Bologna chapter supplies context, not a causal test of these outcomes.
